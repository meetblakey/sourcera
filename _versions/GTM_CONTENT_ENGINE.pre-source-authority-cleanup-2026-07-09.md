## SECTION 1 — CONTENT STRATEGY ARCHITECTURE

### 1.1 The Content Thesis

**Sourcera is defining, naming, and owning the category "Software Evaluation Platform."** Content is not a demand-capture surface on top of a defined market — it is the instrument that brings the category into existence. Every piece of content must do one of three things, or it should not ship:

1. **Define the category.** Make it impossible to describe Sourcera's surface area with any existing category label (procurement suite, RFP tool, review platform, TPRM).
2. **Displace the status quo.** The real competitor is Excel + SharePoint + Outlook + PowerPoint (GTM_POSITIONING §4.1). Every piece makes that workflow visibly insufficient relative to an alternative that already exists.
3. **Seed a compounding surface.** Either feeds a long-tail SEO moat (M9/M10/M11), attaches a named methodology to the founder (The Sourcera Method), or generates artifacts sellers and buyers cite for Sourcera (templates, calculators, readiness assessments).

Content that does none of those three is marketing noise and kills the ≤10-hour/week budget.

**Why category-creation content is the highest-leverage channel for a solo operator.** Sourcera sits in a market without a Gartner Magic Quadrant, without existing budget lines, with buyers describing their need as "we need a better way to evaluate software." Demand-generation advertising against that query pattern is a waste — there is no keyword volume to capture on "Software Evaluation Platform" today. Content has to *teach* buyers the category name before it can capture the demand. Category-defining IP (the Method, the 13 phases, the 3 scoring models, the Selection Record standard) is the shortest path from zero to dominant SERP presence on a category nobody else is targeting.

**Core content asset: The Sourcera Method.** The Method (Master Spec §2 — 13-phase pipeline, FM/PM/EJ scoring, two-phase shortlisting, grade rubric, opinionated constraints) is the founder's publishable IP. Every blog post, LinkedIn post, guest essay, template, calculator, and podcast appearance either references, extends, applies, or teaches it. Within 12 months, the goal is that procurement practitioners have heard of "the Sourcera Method" even if they haven't signed up. This is the same move Vendr made with "SaaS Buying" and Tropic made with "SaaS Spend Optimization" — defining a workflow and naming yourself its canonical reference.

Without the human editorial layer, the Schema.org emissions are naked data; with it, they are a trusted category catalog.

### 2.5 Programmatic SEO — Scaling Via the Automated Surfaces

Programmatic SEO is where Sourcera's automated content surfaces (M9/M10/M11) produce the long-tail moat a solo operator could never write manually. The founder's job is to design, trigger, and editorially govern these surfaces — not to author them.

**M9 — Per-Category Marketplace Landing Pages** (`/categories/:slug`)
*Targets:* The 6 seed categories (Security & GRC, Data & Analytics, DevOps, RevOps, HRIS, AI Infrastructure — §1.3.1 Positioning), each with 4–8 sub-categories. 30–50 initial category pages; 200+ by year 2.
*Kw surface:* `[category] vendors`, `[category] software`, `best [category] tools`, `[category] buyer's guide`.
*Content mix per page:* AI-generated FAQ (§6.16.5 `category_faq_generation`), seller listings (from published Seller Profiles §6.15.1), featured editorial block (links to the human-written category guide), Schema.org `CollectionPage` markup.

**M10 — "How to Evaluate [X]" Guides** (`/guides/how-to-evaluate-:slug`)
*Targets:* Every M9 category gets a paired M10 guide. 30+ guides on launch; 100+ by year 2.
*Content mix:* AI-drafted long-form guide (§6.16.5 `guide_draft_generation`), editorially reviewed on a quarterly refresh (`guide_refresh_analysis`). Each guide teaches the Sourcera Method applied to that category. The founder writes the template for the six seed categories; M10 produces the rest.

**M11 — Software Comparison Pages** (`/compare/:slug1-vs-:slug2`)
*Targets:* Pairwise comparisons across the SellerSoftware entities in priority categories. Target density: top 30 vendors per category × cross-pairs = 400+ pages in year 1 across 6 seed categories.
*Content mix:* AI-generated side-by-side on capabilities, pricing (when public), compliance posture, integration surface. Pulls from SellerSoftware KBs and Capability Declarations.
*Editorial governance:* The founder approves the comparison axes — not the individual pages. Axes are the stylebook.

**M12 — Aggregate Market Intelligence Reports** (`/reports/:quarter`)

## SECTION 7 — CONTENT PRODUCTION SYSTEM

### 7.1 Claude-Assisted Content Workflow

The entire 10-hour/week cadence is only sustainable because the founder uses Claude as a production partner across drafting, editing, repurposing, and distribution. Claude is a co-author and editor — never the primary author. Every shipped piece carries the founder's voice, opinions, and specific examples.

**Workflow per long-form piece (2,500–5,000 words):**

1. **Claude outline (15 min):** Founder prompts Claude with the piece's pillar, keyword cluster, target persona, and the specific product surfaces it references. Claude produces a 10-section outline with the thesis sentence, the hook, and the five sub-arguments. Founder edits in place.
2. **Founder drafts the thesis + hook + first 300 words (30 min):** Voice is set at the top. This is the only section Claude never drafts from scratch.
3. **Claude drafts sections 2–10 (1 hour):** With the founder's opening paragraph as the voice anchor and the outline as scaffolding. Claude draft 1 is ~80% of final length.
4. **Founder edits heavily (2 hours):** Cut fluff. Add specific product references with source citations. Add one real anecdote per section. Replace generic examples with Sourcera examples (Priya, Marcus, Elena, Sam, Leo, Rachel — or the specific regulated-industry narrative). Reject any Claude output that uses "startup clichés" or "genuinely/honestly/straightforward" (GTM_POSITIONING tone guidance).
5. **Claude editorial pass (30 min):** Founder re-prompts Claude with the edited draft asking for adversarial review: "What claims in this piece are weakest? What would a procurement director at a 3,000-person healthcare system push back on?" Founder integrates strongest objections.
6. **Founder final pass (30 min):** SEO check (title tag, meta, internal links), image insertion, preview link test, publish.

**Total per long-form: 4.5 hours authored; Claude acts as leverage of ~50% on drafting and ~25% on editing.**

**Workflow per LinkedIn post (600 words or less):**

1. Founder writes the hook and thesis (10 min).
2. Claude drafts the body (5 min).
3. Founder rewrites in voice (15 min).
4. Post. 30 minutes end-to-end.

**Workflow per newsletter issue:**

1. Founder picks the Lead topic (5 min — usually a reference to an upcoming or recent blog post).
2. Claude drafts 800 words from a 2-sentence brief (15 min).
3. Founder rewrites in voice, adds anecdote, writes CTA (90 min).
4. Founder writes the "This Week on Sourcera" block manually (10 min).
5. Scheduling + preview (10 min). **2 hours total — matches the Tuesday content block.**

**Workflow per gated asset (template, calculator):**

1. Founder defines requirements + UX for the asset (1 hour).
2. Claude drafts the asset content (templates: 2 hours; calculators: founder + Claude code the logic together — 3–5 hours).
3. Founder edits asset content (1 hour).
4. Graphic design via Canva + Figma for PDFs (2 hours).
5. Loops nurture sequence: Claude drafts 5 emails from a 1-paragraph brief (1 hour); founder edits in voice (1 hour).
6. Landing page in Framer/Webflow (1 hour).
7. **Total: ~10 hours per gated asset — spread across one week, sacrificing the long-form cadence for that week.**

### 7.2 Content Repurposing Chain

The single highest content ROI multiplier is repurposing. One cornerstone piece becomes ~15 derivative pieces over 4 weeks without additional authoring.

## APPENDIX A — Content Engine Drift-Prevention Checklist

- [ ] Uses the term "Software Evaluation Platform" or an unambiguous variant.
- [ ] Does not describe Sourcera as a "procurement suite," "RFP platform," "vendor management tool," "AI tool for procurement," or any other adjacent-category label.
- [ ] References at least one of: 13-phase pipeline, Dual-Console, Selection Record, the Sourcera Method, outcome-accounted AI pricing, unlimited seats.

**Pricing accuracy (any pricing mention):**
- [ ] Buyer plan prices: $0 / $299 / $799 / $1,999 / Enterprise custom ($3K floor). Monthly rates only used in the distinct "monthly" context.
- [ ] Seller plan prices: $0 / $149 / $499 / $1,499 / Enterprise custom ($3K floor).
- [ ] AI budgets: buyer $5 / $50 / $300 / $800 / Committed; seller $5 / $30 / $200 / $600 / Committed. Denominated in value-dollars.
- [ ] Unlimited seats mentioned where pricing appears.

**Audience tagging (buyer vs. seller):**
- [ ] Content is explicitly tagged for buyer, seller, or cross-side audience.
- [ ] Buyer-facing content does not accidentally describe seller-side workflows as buyer-visible.
- [ ] Seller-facing content distinguishes Forced-Signup and Organic paths where relevant.

**Automated-surface linkage (where applicable):**
- [ ] If the content covers a category, it links to the M9 category page.
- [ ] If the content covers a comparison, it links to the relevant M11 `/compare/` URL.
- [ ] If the content covers evaluation methodology, it links to the relevant M10 guide.
- [ ] If the content references market data, it cites M12 or M13 explicitly.

**Voice and tone:**
- [ ] No use of "genuinely," "honestly," "straightforward."
- [ ] No startup clichés ("game-changer," "revolutionize," "disrupt," "10x").
- [ ] No hand-wavy AI language ("AI magic," "AI-native," "cognitive AI").
- [ ] No competitor attack framing. Reposition, don't attack.
- [ ] One distinct founder opinion per piece, stated plainly.

**Citation and source discipline:**
- [ ] Every capability reference cites its Master Spec section.
- [ ] Every pricing claim cites Master Spec §34.
- [ ] Every growth mechanic cites its M-number (M1–M17).
- [ ] Anonymized customer narratives do not name real customers without consent.

**Distribution discipline:**
- [ ] Has a planned derivative — will be repurposed into at least 3 other formats over the next 14 days.
- [ ] Has a specific CTA. One, not three.
- [ ] Links to the newsletter subscription at least once.
- [ ] Links to either a Sourcera product surface or a gated asset, with the CTA routed appropriately.
